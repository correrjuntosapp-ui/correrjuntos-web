Beginner Running Kit for 200€

A closed shopping list that adds up to exactly 200€. Everything you need to start running without overspending — or underspending. Updated May 2026.

Taking up running should not drain your bank account. The problem is that the moment you walk into a specialist shop or open Amazon, you get hit with 500€ watches, 250€ carbon-plate shoes and "premium" gels at 4€ a pop. You don't need any of it.

This guide is a closed list: 8 specific products, 200€ in total, everything you need for your first 6-12 months of running. No duplication, no inflated marketing. The rule is simple — spend where it matters (shoes) and save where nobody notices (t-shirt, socks, accessories).

Before you start: If you have never run in your life, read how to start running (beginner's guide) first and then come back here. Without the basics, gear won't get you very far.

1. Shoes (90€) — the most important investment

Half the budget goes here because this is the only item that affects your health directly. A bad shoe can give you plantar fasciitis, shin splints or joint injuries that cost 800-1500€ in physiotherapy. Spending 90€ on the right shoes is pure prevention.

One warning though: do NOT buy premium noise-cancelling headphones (they cut you off from traffic, which is dangerous). Go for bone conduction instead (your ears stay open) or basic open-fit sports headphones.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Shoes that are too expensive on day one: carbon plate, PEBA foam, premium everything. You don't need them and they wear out faster (300-500 km vs 700-800 km for normal shoes).
  • Cotton socks: guaranteed blisters. Bin the cotton.
  • A 600€ GPS watch before you know you'll stick with it: wait three months. If you are still running then, it is worth it. If not, you just saved 600€.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones on the road: dangerous. You cannot hear cars.
  • Buying supplements you don't need: a normal diet covers 90% of your needs. Magnesium and vitamin D only if a blood test shows a deficiency.
  • Branded t-shirts for status: 80€ on a Nike Pro versus 15€ on a Kalenji = the same wicking. The difference is marketing.

Frequently asked questions

Is it realistic to start running with only 200€ of gear?

Yes. Shoes are 50-60% of the budget (the one thing you should NOT cut corners on) and the rest is gear where the gap between mid-range and premium brands is marginal. Spend 200€ sensibly and you are covered for 6-12 months of running.

Why do the shoes take up so much of the budget?

Because they are the only thing that affects your health directly. A bad shoe causes plantar fasciitis or joint injuries = 800-1500€ in physiotherapy. Spending 90€ on the right shoes is pure prevention.

Do I need a GPS watch to get started?

No. Phone apps (Strava, CorrerJuntos) track your GPS for free, accurately enough for the first 3-6 months. When you want precise metrics (cadence, VO2 max), step up to a Garmin FR 165 or COROS PACE 3 (250-320€).

Can I buy the whole kit at Decathlon instead of Amazon?

Yes. Decathlon's Kalenji/Kiprun range is genuinely decent, from 7-90€. Decathlon's edge: trying the shoes on. Amazon's edge: speed and comparisons. Same price bracket.

How often should I replace this kit?

Shoes: every 600-800 km (6-12 months). Technical clothing: 12-18 months. Socks: 6-8 months. Headphones and accessories: 2-3 years. Keeping it in good shape prevents injuries.
