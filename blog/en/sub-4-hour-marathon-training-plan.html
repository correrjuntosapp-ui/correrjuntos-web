Sub 4 Hour Marathon Training Plan: 16 Weeks

The complete plan to break four hours in the marathon. Paces, key sessions and race-day strategy for club runners who want to smash that psychological barrier. Updated May 2026.

Four hours is the most famous psychological barrier in recreational running. It is not elite — hundreds of thousands of runners around the world chase it every year — but it is not easy either. You need a plan, a strategy and 16 weeks of consistent work.

This plan is built for runners who already have a solid base and want to make the jump. You do not have to be a professional athlete, but you do have to meet a few minimum requirements.

Prerequisites

Breaking four hours is within reach for a club runner, but you need a real base underneath you. Check these boxes before you start:

  • Half marathon under 1:55 — If you can cover 21K in less than 1h55, you have the aerobic base you need. Under 1:50 gives you a comfortable margin.
  • 10K under 53 minutes — It tells you your cruising speed is good enough for the target.
  • 35-40+ km per week over the last 2-3 months — Your body has to be adapted to the volume before you begin.
  • 4-5 days a week available — The plan is built around 4 key sessions. Without them, the goal is not realistic.
Not there yet? Start with our half marathon training plan first. Once you go under 1:55, you are ready for this one.

Your Training Paces

For a sub 4 hour marathon (target pace: 5:41/km), these are the paces you will work with over the 16 weeks. Respecting them is the single most important part of the plan — the most common mistake is running the easy days too fast:

  • Easy running (Z2): 7:00-7:30/km — 70-80% of your volume. You should be able to hold a full conversation. If you cannot, you are going too fast.
  • Steady running: 6:20-6:40/km — A little livelier, for moderate volume days (10-14 km).
  • Tempo/threshold: 5:55-6:10/km — Sustained effort. You can speak in short sentences. It builds your ability to hold a demanding pace.
  • Intervals (VO2max): 5:15-5:30/km — 600m-1000m reps with active recovery. They develop speed and running economy.
  • Marathon pace (MP): 5:38-5:45/km — The pace you will race at. It should feel comfortably hard in blocks of 8-12 km.

To control those paces precisely, a GPS running watch is essential. At this level, running on feel with no pace feedback is running blind.

The 4 Key Weekly Sessions

Every week of the plan revolves around 4 sessions you cannot skip. Everything else is easy running or rest:

1. Long run (Sunday)

The most important session of the week. It starts at 20 km and builds to 30-32 km in the peak weeks. The pace is easy (7:00-7:15/km) almost every time, except for the long runs with marathon-pace blocks in phases 2 and 3.

2. Intervals (Tuesday)

These build your VO2max and running economy. Examples: 8x600m at 5:20/km with 90s recovery, or 5x1000m at 5:25/km with 2 min jog. The key is respecting the recovery so the quality holds.

3. Tempo run (Thursday)

This one builds your ability to hold a sustained effort. It ranges from 20 min continuous at threshold pace (6:05/km) to 2x4 km with 3 min recovery. It is the session that prepares you best for the last 12 km of the marathon.

4. Steady run with a progression (Saturday)

45-55 min starting easy and finishing the last 8-10 min at marathon pace. It teaches your body to run at goal pace with the weekend's accumulated fatigue.

Golden rule: If you have to cut something in a difficult week, cut the easy run on Wednesday or Friday. Never sacrifice the 4 key sessions. Quality beats volume.

Down weeks: Weeks 4, 8, 11 and 14-16 are cutback weeks. Drop total volume by 25-30% but keep the paces on the key sessions. Supercompensation — where you actually improve — happens during recovery.

Race Day Strategy

A sub 4 marathon is won or lost in the first 10 km. The adrenaline, the crowds and good weather push everyone out too fast. A negative split (second half equal to or slightly faster than the first) is the most effective approach for club runners:

  • Km 1-10: Go out at 5:45-5:50/km. It will feel very easy — that is exactly the point. Keep your ego in check. Every second you bank here can cost you 30 seconds after km 30.
  • Km 10-21: Settle into 5:40-5:42/km. You are on target pace now. If you feel great, do not push: wait.
  • Km 21-30: Hold 5:40/km. This is where the mental race starts. Focus on the next kilometre, not on the ones still to come.
  • Km 30-35: The wall. If you have been conservative, you will pass through it fine. If you went out too fast, this is where you find out. Take the km 32 gel and keep your form together.
  • Km 35-42: Empty the tank. Running 5:35/km over those last kilometres gives you plenty of margin for the sub 4. You will pass hundreds of people who went out too hard.
The km 5 trap: If you hit 5 km at 5:20-5:25/km and "feel great", you are in self-destruct mode. Slow down. The marathon does not start until km 30.

Nutrition and Gels

At sub 4 hour pace, your glycogen stores run dry somewhere between km 28 and km 33 if you do not refuel. A good nutrition strategy can be worth 10-15 minutes:

  • Carb loading (3 days out): Push carbohydrates up to 7-9 g per kg of body weight. Pasta, rice, bread, bananas. Cut back on fat and fibre. Read our guide to carb loading for the marathon.
  • Pre-race breakfast (3h before): Oats, white bread with jam, a banana and coffee. Nothing you have not tried before. Race morning is no time for experiments.
  • Gels during the race: 4-5 gels, one every 35-40 minutes from km 8. Sample plan: km 8, km 16, km 24, km 32. If there are aid stations at those points, use their water to wash the gel down. See which energy gel to use in a marathon.
  • Hydration: A cup of water (150-200 ml) at every aid station (roughly every 5 km). Do not skip one even if you are not thirsty. At the 10 km stations, alternate with a sports drink.
  • Salts: If it is warmer than 18°C, take a salt tablet every hour. It reduces cramping and fluid retention.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Going out too fast: Mistake number one. If your first 5 km are under 5:30/km, you are putting the last 12 km at risk. Adrenaline lies.
  • Skipping the down weeks: More is not better. Weeks 4, 8 and 11 are cutback weeks by design. Skipping them builds chronic fatigue that will come back to bite you.
  • Running the long runs too fast: The long run is about time on your feet at an easy pace, not about setting records. Only the last long runs include marathon-pace blocks.
  • Not training with your gels: Your stomach trains too. If you take gels on race day without testing them, you are risking stomach trouble at km 25.
  • Debuting your race shoes on race day: Use them in at least 3-4 quality sessions beforehand so your feet adapt.
  • Ignoring the taper: In the last 2 weeks you will feel like you are losing fitness. That is normal and necessary. Your body is recharging. Do not add extra sessions to "make up" for it.
