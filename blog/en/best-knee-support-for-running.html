The 3 types of knee support for running

Before we talk about specific products, you need to understand that "knee support" is not one single thing. There are three types and each one solves a different problem. Using the wrong one does not just fail to help, it can make the injury worse.

Type 1: Compression sleeve

An elastic tube that slides up the leg and wraps the whole knee. Usual materials: nylon and spandex, thin neoprene or knitted compression fabric. It applies even 360° pressure across the joint. What it is for: IT band syndrome (pain on the outer side), chondromalacia (pain under the kneecap when going down stairs), diffuse knee pain with no clear origin, and general prevention during hard training. Advantage for running: it is the least intrusive on long runs because it has no edges that rub and creates no pressure points.

Type 2: Patellar strap

A narrow neoprene band that wraps the knee just below the kneecap. It applies targeted pressure on the patellar tendon to reduce the tension during flexion and extension. What it is for: patellar tendinitis (specific pain just below the kneecap, especially running downhill or going down stairs) and Osgood-Schlatter syndrome. Important: the strap has to sit in exactly the right place to work. Placed over the kneecap or too low, it does nothing.

Type 3: Hinged brace (with patella opening or side hinges)

A stiffer structure in neoprene or technical fabric that can include metal side stays, an opening for the kneecap or extra fastening straps. What it is for: ligament instability, post knee surgery, severe chondromalacia, mild to moderate arthritis during exercise. Downside for running: these are the heaviest and the hottest. For long-distance running they can get uncomfortable. For runners with a diagnosed injury and treatment under way, they are the option.

Important warning: A knee support relieves and holds the joint during exercise, but it is not a treatment. If your knee pain has lasted more than 72 hours, gets worse when you run or comes with visible swelling, the first step is always a physiotherapist or sports doctor. The support can be part of your return to training, not a substitute for the diagnosis.

What to look for before buying a knee support for running

1. It must not slide down mid-run

The most common problem with cheap knee supports: by kilometre 2 they are around your ankle. Look for non-slip silicone on the top and bottom edges, especially on sleeves. Hinged braces with velcro straps are safer. Patellar straps with a metal buckle (not velcro alone) hold pressure better through the stride.

2. Breathability of the material

Neoprene traps a lot of heat and holds sweat. For summer running or long runs, knitted fabrics or technical mesh are far more comfortable. The difference between wearing a neoprene support for 10 km in August and wearing a compression knit is noticeable.

3. The right size (this matters more than it sounds)

A sleeve that is too tight can restrict circulation. Too loose and it gives no compression and slides down. Measure the circumference of your knee at kneecap height. Most brands have their own size chart, so always check the one for that specific product before buying.

4. Which specific problem it is for

I explained this above, but I am repeating it because it is the most important part: the type of support has to match the type of pain. A compression sleeve does little for patellar tendinitis. A patellar strap does little for IT band pain. If you do not know what you have, start with a compression sleeve (less risk of using it wrong) and go see a physio.

Which one to choose for your problem

Patellar tendinitis (pain below the kneecap running downhill)

You need a patellar strap. The options: CAMBIVO Strap (2-pack, more padded) or bonmedico (more discreet, better for racing). The strap has to sit just below the kneecap, not over it. If you also have swelling in the knee, add a compression sleeve over the strap.

IT band syndrome (pain on the outer side of the knee)

A compression sleeve. The even pressure it applies reduces the friction of the IT band as it passes over the lateral femoral condyle. Options: Bodyprox 2-Pack or FEVAN 2-Pack. Pair it with IT band stretching (foam roller on the outside of the thigh) and a temporary cut in mileage.

Patellar chondromalacia (diffuse pain under the kneecap, worse on stairs)

A support with a gel ring, or a hinged brace that centres the kneecap. First option: CAMBIVO with Gel. If the chondromalacia is severe or you have instability: ABYON ELITE or Zamst RK-1 (if your physio recommends it).

Prevention during hard training (no active injury)

A light compression sleeve. The most efficient option: FEVAN 2-Pack at €10 or Bodyprox 2-Pack at €14. There is no need for a hinged brace for prevention if you do not have ligament instability.

Return to training after a meniscus or ligament injury

Always under a physio's supervision. If the physio clears you to run with a support: ABYON ELITE or Zamst RK-1 Plus. Do not run with a meniscus or ligament injury without a medical diagnosis and clearance to go back to exercise.

Runner's tip: A knee support does not fix the root cause. What does fix the root cause is quad and hamstring strength work, the right footwear, and cutting mileage temporarily. The support is a bridge that lets you keep training while you treat the cause. If you have been wearing it on every run for more than 2-3 weeks and the pain is not improving, it is time to stop and have a physio look at the whole kinetic chain (foot, ankle, knee, hip).

Frequently asked questions

What kind of knee support do I need to run with knee pain?

It depends on the problem. Patellar tendinitis (pain below the kneecap when running downhill): a patellar strap such as the CAMBIVO or the bonmedico. IT band syndrome (pain on the outer side of the knee): a compression sleeve. Chondromalacia or diffuse pain: a sleeve with a gel ring, or a hinged brace. If you do not know what you have, see a physiotherapist before buying.

Can I keep running with a knee support on?

If the pain is mild (1-3/10), running with a support while you treat the cause with a physio is reasonable. If the pain is moderate to severe (4+/10) or gets worse during the run, stop and see a doctor. A knee support is not a substitute for a diagnosis.

What is the difference between a compression sleeve and a patellar strap?

The sleeve wraps the whole knee with even pressure (useful for IT band pain and diffuse pain). The strap is a narrow band that applies targeted pressure below the kneecap (specific to patellar tendinitis). They are different products for different problems.

How do I know what size to buy?

Measure the circumference of your knee at kneecap height. The usual pattern: S = 33-36 cm, M = 36-40 cm, L = 40-44 cm, XL = 44-48 cm. If you fall between sizes, size up for sleeves (more comfort) and size down for straps (more pressure). Check each brand's own size chart.

How long can I wear a knee support while running?

Compression sleeves: the whole run, even 2-3 hours. Patellar straps: take them off when you finish, because constant pressure can irritate the skin. Neoprene hinged braces: they trap heat, so in summer and on long runs they can get uncomfortable. If the pain increases during the run with the support on, stop.

Is it worth paying more for a brand like Zamst?

It depends. The €10-15 products (Bodyprox, NativoDex, FEVAN) work perfectly well for mild pain or as prevention for runners with no active injury. If you have an injury diagnosed by a physio and you are going to wear the support on every run for months, the difference in material quality, durability and fit precision of a Zamst (€73) or a NEENCA (€50) is noticeable. Zamst in particular is a brand used by sports physiotherapists. If the pain is serious and the support is part of your treatment, spending €30-70 makes sense. If it is just occasional prevention, a €10-25 model is enough.

Can a knee support make my injury worse?

Yes, if you use it badly. A sleeve that is too tight restricts circulation. A strap in the wrong position does not help and can add pressure where it should not. Using a support to mask pain that should be making you stop can aggravate the underlying injury. A knee support is temporary help, not a definitive treatment.
